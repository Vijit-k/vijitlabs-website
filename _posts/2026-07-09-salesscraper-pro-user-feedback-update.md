Most founders say they want feedback. Few actually go get it.

It's easy to wait for feedback to arrive on its own — a support email, a churn survey, a one-star review. It's much rarer to go looking for it before something breaks, from someone who has no obligation to answer you at all.

I've been doing that on LinkedIn for a while now, and twice this year it directly changed a product I ship. The most recent one just shipped in SalesScraper Pro. Here's the actual pattern, not the polished version.

## The habit: cold-DM people who'd actually know

I don't send cold DMs to sell. I send them to ask questions I can't answer myself, to people whose job puts them closer to the problem than I am.

Earlier this year, that meant messaging a support leader I'd never met, about a specific operational headache EscalationAI was meant to solve. That conversation didn't just validate the idea — it reshaped how the product actually behaves, in a way I wouldn't have found sitting inside my own head guessing at what support teams deal with. I've kept that person's input anonymous by their preference, but the product is better because of it, and it's live today.

## The most recent one: SalesScraper Pro

The same pattern played out again with SalesScraper Pro. Anand Joshi flagged something specific after actually using the tool against his own LinkedIn network — the kind of real-world edge case you only find by putting a tool in someone else's hands and watching where it breaks, not by testing it yourself against your own predictable data.

I'm deliberately not going into the technical detail of what changed here. Some of it touches how the tool interacts with LinkedIn's platform, and getting specific about mechanics isn't something I want to put in writing publicly. What I can say: the fix is in the update we just submitted to the Chrome Web Store, and it makes the tool noticeably more reliable in exactly the situation Anand ran into.

Thank you, Anand — the feedback mattered, and the product is better for it.

## Why this works better than most feedback channels

Founders default to two feedback sources: people who already paid, who are biased toward liking you enough to have stuck around, and people who complain loudly, who are biased toward the worst experience rather than the typical one. Both are real signal. Both are incomplete.

A cold DM to someone with no relationship to your product yet gets you something different — a stranger's honest first read, with nothing to protect and no reason to be polite about it. Most people ignore cold DMs, understandably. But the ones who respond tend to give you exactly the unfiltered input a customer who already likes you won't bother giving.

## The actual takeaway

If you're building something and relying on your own instincts, or on whatever feedback happens to land in your inbox, you're working with a narrower sample than you think.

Find a handful of people on LinkedIn who'd actually know something about the problem you're solving — not random connections, people whose day job puts them closer to it than you are. Send them a specific question, not a pitch. Most won't answer. The ones who do will tell you something you couldn't have guessed on your own.
